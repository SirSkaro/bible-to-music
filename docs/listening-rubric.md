# Review a Bible Music take

This is the producer-owned listening rubric for the targeted reviewer. The runtime supplies a selected revision with the brief, arrangement and audio revision. The producer can develop the criteria here as real examples become available. A useful entry answers “What did you hear, why did it matter for memorization, and what did you do with this take?” A short pair of recordings with a clear difference can teach more than a page of adjectives.

## Using the rubric on a take

Listen to the complete mix. For an ambiguous passage, audition the surrounding phrase. Use a vocal stem as supplementary evidence when available. Record the exact revision, what was audible, the expected placement, the evidence source and a time window with its precision. Mark a criterion unscored until the producer supplies its decision rule. Apply only the producer’s recorded rules to eligibility and ranking. Keep the producer’s final choice separate from the review.

## One entry per judgment

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Criterion and context | Name the issue and the arrangement or style in which it matters. |
| Audible cue | Describe what a listener should attend to in the final mix. Include an acceptable counterexample when a similar sound would be intentional. |
| Example | Give a neutral recording ID, its revision and the verified phrase window. Mark timing unknown until someone has checked it. |
| Producer decision | Record keep, repair, try another take, or audition again. State what tipped the decision. Identify any rule that makes a take ineligible, and any preference that only affects ranking. |
| Uncertainty | State when to listen in context, consult an isolated vocal, or leave the call for the producer. Record what the mix actually supports. |

## Two starting examples from the project

- **“Fulfillment.”** The producer heard “foo-fillment” even though a transcriber might return the expected spelling. A targeted listener should report how the transition into the second syllable sounds and where to audition it. The missing consonant, attempted edit and exact audio revision are different evidence. Add a labeled original/edited pair if available. The severity cutoff and whether a particular edit works are the producer's labels.
- **Echo or missing lead?** An echo can be planned in an arrangement. Listen for whether the intended lead words remain intelligible while the backing part repeats them. An example where both lines are audible and one where a key word disappears would give this criterion a concrete boundary.

Useful later entries include the transition into a clipped ending, a hook that aids recall, and a genre treatment that overwhelms the words. Capture a real keep/repair decision and the producer's reason before turning any of these into a ranking preference.

## Review contract

The first blind pass receives audio and a neutral ID. The targeted reviewer receives the audio revision, approved arrangement, producer brief, active rubric, and any proposed region to inspect. For each finding return the criterion, audible evidence, approximate start and end when supported, uncertainty, and a suggested audition or repair. Attribute the evidence to the full mix or the isolated vocal. Keep the human answer key separate from both inputs.

The application can validate the shape of a finding and link it to playback. The producer supplies any eligibility thresholds, musical priorities and weighting. Save the rubric version on the review. An uncalibrated observation can still save audition time.
